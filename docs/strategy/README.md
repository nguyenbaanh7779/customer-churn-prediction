# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Project Background

Retail businesses rely on customer relationships and repeat purchases to maintain sustainable revenue. However, customers may gradually reduce their purchasing activity or stop purchasing altogether.

Identifying these customers before they become inactive can be challenging, particularly when businesses have large customer bases and substantial transaction histories. Without an effective way to identify potential churn, businesses may miss opportunities to maintain customer relationships and encourage repeat purchases.

Customer churn prediction can help businesses better understand customer purchasing behavior and identify customers who may be at risk of becoming inactive.

## 1.2 Problem Statement

- **Difficulty in early identification of churn risk.** Retail businesses struggle to detect customers who are likely to stop purchasing based on historical transaction behavior. Traditional methods often identify inactive customers only after a long period of inactivity, by which time retention opportunities are already limited.
- **Limited retention resources impacting revenue growth.** The business needs to increase sales revenue but operates under constrained marketing and retention resources. Without a data-driven way to prioritize at-risk customers, retention efforts may be inefficient and fail to maximize revenue.

## 1.3 Research Questions

1. How can transaction history be converted into customer-level data for churn prediction?
2. How well does purchasing behavior predict churn?
3. Does churn-risk ranking improve prioritization under limited retention resources compared to rule-based targeting?
4. Which customer behaviors are most associated with churn? (Correlation, not causation.)

## 1.4 Project Objectives

To develop a data-driven churn prediction model that identifies customers at risk of leaving based on their purchasing history, enabling retail e-commerce businesses to prioritize limited retention resources and maximize sales revenue.

## 1.5 Scope and Limitations

**In scope:** existing-customer retention, churn prediction, customer snapshots, weekly batch scoring, risk ranking and Top-K targeting.

**Out of scope / not claimed:**

- Causal uplift and incremental campaign revenue — the transaction data contain no randomized treatment/control information.
- A live deployment. **Chapters 8 and 9 describe a proposed operational design**; they are not evaluated on a running system.
- Profit analysis based on real margins or costs — these are not in the data and enter only as stated assumptions.

# 2. Problem Statement and Approach

## 2.1 E-commerce Sales Business

The business sells products through an e-commerce transaction system, and sales generate revenue from customer purchases. At a high level:

::: {.formula}
Profit = Sales Revenue − Business Costs
:::

Costs may include product/operating costs, marketing costs and retention incentive costs.

## 2.2 Revenue from New and Existing Customers

Sales revenue can be generated by both new customers making their first purchases and existing customers making repeat purchases. New customers contribute to business growth by expanding the customer base, while existing customers may generate additional revenue through continued purchasing activity.

## 2.3 Customer Churn as a Business Problem

When existing customers gradually reduce their purchasing activity or stop purchasing altogether, businesses may lose potential future sales. Understanding the behavior of these customers can help businesses identify opportunities to encourage repeat purchases.

## 2.4 Retention and Marketing Cost Constraints

Retention activities require resources. The company cannot necessarily apply the same retention action to every existing customer because marketing budget, campaign capacity and incentives are limited. Retention resources must be allocated selectively.

## 2.5 Business Objectives

The primary business objective is to support sustainable sales revenue by identifying existing customers who are at risk of churn and enabling more informed customer retention decisions. The specific objectives are:

- **Identify at-risk customers:** recognize existing customers who are likely to stop purchasing within a predefined period.
- **Understand purchasing behavior:** investigate customer characteristics, such as purchase frequency, monetary value and time since the last purchase, that may be associated with churn risk.
- **Support customer prioritization:** provide insights that help the business prioritize customers for potential retention activities.

## 2.6 Success Criteria

Success is defined **relative to simple alternatives** rather than by arbitrary absolute thresholds:

| Level | Criterion (evaluated on the unseen test period) |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Predictive | Final model PR-AUC exceeds the test-period churn rate (random reference) and the recency-rule baseline. |
| Targeting | Final model Lift\@K and Recall\@K at the chosen operating K (e.g. 20–30%) exceed the recency-rule baseline, averaged over weekly scoring dates. |
| Stability | Top-K performance does not collapse on individual test weeks (report mean ± standard deviation). |
| Business scenario | Under the base scenario, estimated net impact at the chosen K is positive and higher than for recency-rule and random targeting at the same K. |

## 2.7 From Business Objective to Analytical Problem

Increasing revenue requires more repeat-purchase revenue from existing customers and efficient use of retention resources. The analytical direction is therefore to identify existing customers at risk of not purchasing again, estimate churn probability, and prioritize customers for retention activities.

## 2.8 Customer Retention Strategy

::: {.flow}
Eligible Existing Customers → Churn Prediction (estimate churn probability) → Risk Ranking (rank customers by predicted churn risk) → Business Rules and Available Capacity → Target Customers for Retention (prioritize customers for potential campaigns)
:::

## 2.9 Implementation Roadmap

The approach is implemented as a sequence of steps, each covered by one chapter:

- **Understand the data** (Chapter 4) — sales and customer behavior, purchase intervals and revenue by purchase cadence, which inform the prediction horizon H and the activity threshold R~max~.
- **Prepare customer snapshots** (Chapter 5) — turn transactions into Customer × Snapshot Date rows with point-in-time features, churn labels and an embargoed temporal split.
- **Model churn risk** (Chapter 6) — compare baselines and algorithms and select a final model.
- **Evaluate** (Chapter 7) — predictive, ranking and scenario-based business-impact evaluation on the unseen test period.
- **Deploy** (Chapter 8) — weekly batch scoring and Top-K targeting, as a proposed operational design.

# 3. Dataset and Data Specification

## 3.1 Data Source and Collection

The project uses the publicly available **Online Retail II** dataset as a historical extract of e-commerce transaction records. The dataset is obtained and loaded into the analytical environment for data inspection, preprocessing and customer churn analysis. It contains transaction-level information, including invoice identifiers, customer identifiers, product codes, quantities, prices and invoice dates.

## 3.2 Dataset Characteristics and Limitations

The dataset covers the period from December 2009 to December 2011. Its relatively limited historical coverage constrains the number of customer snapshots and future observation periods available for churn labeling (see 5.9). In addition, the retailer serves both individual customers and wholesalers, resulting in substantial differences in purchasing frequency and transaction value. Seasonal demand, particularly around Christmas, may also influence purchasing patterns and should be considered when designing temporal validation and evaluating model performance.

## 3.3 Raw Data Schema

The raw dataset is a single flat transaction table (not pre-split into separate customer, order and product tables): each row is one product line within one invoice.

| Column | Type | Description |
|--------------|----------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `Invoice` | string | Invoice number. A "C"-prefixed value (e.g. `C536379`) marks a cancellation of an earlier invoice; `Quantity` is negative on these rows. |
| `StockCode` | string | Product code. Most codes are numeric (e.g. `85123A`); a small set of non-numeric codes are not products at all (3.5). |
| `Description` | string | Free-text product description; can be missing or inconsistent for the same `StockCode`. |
| `Quantity` | integer | Units on this line. Negative for cancellations. |
| `InvoiceDate` | datetime | Timestamp of the transaction line. |
| `Price` | float | Unit price (GBP). |
| `Customer ID` | float | Customer identifier. Missing for a meaningful share of rows (5.1) — these cannot be attached to any customer history. |
| `Country` | string | Customer's country, self-reported at checkout. |

## 3.4 Pipeline Column Names

The raw columns are renamed to snake_case names used throughout the pipeline: `invoice_id`, `product_id`, `product_description`, `quantity`, `invoice_time`, `unit_price`, `customer_id` and `country`. Two columns are derived: `total_price` = `quantity` × `unit_price`, and `invoice_month` (`invoice_time` truncated to the first of its month, used for monthly aggregation).

## 3.5 Value Conventions

- **Cancellation invoices.** An `invoice_id` starting with "C" marks a cancellation of a previous purchase, with negative `quantity`. These rows are excluded from purchase history (5.2) but kept separately to build the cancellation features (5.6).
- **Non-product stock codes.** A small number of `product_id` values are operational/accounting entries rather than products — postage (`POST`, `DOT`), manual adjustments (`M`), carriage (`C2`), discounts (`D`), samples (`S`), bank charges and similar. They are discovered empirically (5.1) and excluded from product- and customer-level analysis.
- **Missing Customer ID.** Rows without a `customer_id` cannot be attached to any customer history and are excluded from every customer-level analysis from 4.3 onward, although they are still counted in the raw totals of 4.2.

## 3.6 Information Not Available

The dataset contains transactions only. Product cost, product category hierarchy and margin are not available, and neither is any marketing or campaign information (campaign dates, channels, treatment/control assignment, discounts, voucher or contact costs, campaign outcomes). A production system would ideally collect these; in this project, the related quantities used for business-impact estimation in Chapter 7 (margin, contact cost, incentive cost, retention effectiveness) are explicit assumptions.

## 3.7 Analytical Dataset Design

Transactions are not modeled directly. Each final modeling row represents a **Customer × Snapshot Date**: the customer's historical features computed only from information available up to the snapshot date, together with a future churn label. This dataset is built in Chapter 5.

# 5. Data Preparation

## 5.1 Data Cleaning

Clean transaction records using documented, reproducible rules. Report the per-rule data-quality breakdown measured on the raw data (missing Customer ID, exact duplicates, invalid Quantity/Price, cancellations, non-product stock codes) and the rows and customers kept after combining all rules.

## 5.2 Valid Transaction Definition

A **valid purchase** row must satisfy all of the following:

| Rule | Treatment |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Customer ID present | Rows without Customer ID are excluded from customer history (still counted in 4.2 overview). |
| Not a cancellation | Invoices starting with "C" are excluded from purchases; kept separately for cancellation features. |
| Quantity > 0 and Price > 0 | Otherwise excluded. |
| Product stock code | Non-product codes (e.g. postage, manual adjustments, bank charges, marketplace fees) are excluded; the final list is documented. |
| Exact duplicates | Removed. |

A **purchase occasion** is one distinct invoice (or one customer-day, if chosen and documented). Frequency and inter-purchase intervals are computed on purchase occasions, not rows.

## 5.3 Customer History Construction

Aggregate valid transaction history by customer while preserving timestamps and product information.

## 5.4 Eligibility Construction

Eligibility is built in two steps.

**Step 1 — Weekly active-customer base.** At each weekly date *t*, a customer is active if:

1. at least one valid purchase exists on or before *t* (existing customer);
2. Recency at *t* ≤ R~max~ (activity condition);
3. *t* lies within the snapshot period (after warm-up; *t + H* ≤ data end for labeled rows).

**Step 2 — Population flags on the base table.**

| Flag | Rule |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| `in_training_sample` | Row selected by the eligibility-episode rule (5.4). Computed per customer in date order. |
| `in_scoring_population` | Row is active and the customer is not under suppression at *t*. In the historical data there are no real campaigns, so suppression is empty and every active row is in the Scoring Population (see 7.1). |
| `sample_weight` | 1 / (number of weekly active rows of the customer in the training period); used only by the ablation scheme (6.10). |

**Training sample — eligibility-episode rule.** Consecutive weekly snapshots of a customer are near-duplicates with overlapping label windows, so the Training Population is sampled per customer: take a row at the first active week *t*, then no further training row until the first active week ≥ *t* + C (C = H = 90 days); a customer who exceeds R~max~ and later returns starts a new episode. Training labels then have non-overlapping windows and each customer contributes a similar number of rows. The rule applies **only to training** — validation, test and scoring keep every active row. Alternative scheme: all active rows with weight 1 / n~i~ (6.10).

Check the sensitivity of population size and churn rate to R~max~ (e.g. P90 versus P95).

## 5.5 Customer Snapshot Construction

Construct **one weekly active-customer base table** of Customer × Snapshot Date rows, then compute features and labels once for this table. The Training Population and the Scoring Population are filters on it (via the flags above), which guarantees that both use identical feature and label definitions.

Compare weekly all-active and weekly episode-based snapshots (rows, rows per customer) to justify the training-only episode rule, and report the **weekly Scoring Population size** over time — the pool from which marketing selects Top-K each week.

## 5.6 Feature Engineering

Generate historical features using information available up to the snapshot date only.

## 5.7 Churn Label Construction

Use only the prediction window (t, t + H] to determine churn, applying the same valid-purchase definition as 5.2.

**Label EDA.** Report the overall churn rate early — churn may be the *majority* class, which changes how PR-AUC and Precision should be read. Compare the label balance of the training sample and the Scoring Population, show the churn rate by snapshot month (seasonality), and churn by point-in-time frequency and monetary value.

## 5.8 Leakage Prevention

No future information may be used in feature construction. The prediction window is reserved for label construction. Aggregates that depend on the whole dataset (scalers, encoders, imputation values, percentile thresholds) are fitted on the training period only.

## 5.9 Temporal Dataset Split with Embargo

Split chronologically by snapshot date into train, validation and test, with an **embargo of H days** between consecutive periods:

::: {.formula}
first validation date ≥ last training date + H  
first test date ≥ last validation date + H
:::

Without the embargo, labels of late training snapshots are observed during the validation period, so the model effectively learns from a period it is later evaluated on.

| Split | Rows used | Purpose |
|------------|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Train | `in_training_sample` rows in the train period | Fit candidate models |
| Validation | `in_scoring_population` rows in the validation period | Tuning, model selection, calibration fit |
| Final refit | `in_training_sample` rows re-derived over train + validation | Fit the selected model |
| Test | `in_scoring_population` rows in the test period | Final, operation-like evaluation |

Validation and test therefore measure performance exactly as the model would be used: ranking all active customers each week.

::: {.note}
**Illustrative timeline (H = 90 days, weekly Mondays, ≈ 6-month warm-up).** Labelable snapshots run from 2010-06-07 to 2011-09-05 (66 weekly dates).

| Period | Snapshot dates | Weekly dates |
|------------|------------------------------|------------|
| Train | 2010-06-07 → 2010-11-15 | 24 |
| *Embargo* | *90 days* | *—* |
| Validation | 2011-02-14 → 2011-04-04 | 8 |
| *Embargo* | *90 days* | *—* |
| Test | 2011-07-04 → 2011-09-05 | 10 |

About 24 of the 66 weekly dates are consumed by embargoes. If the test period is too short, options are a shorter H, a shorter warm-up, or rolling-origin validation within the training period instead of a separate validation block. The final choice is documented.
:::

**Feasibility check:** for each candidate H, count labelable weekly scoring dates and the train/validation/test weeks left after the embargoes. The test period should contain enough scoring dates (e.g. ≥ 8) for stable Top-K estimates.

Report the rows actually used per split: episode-sampled rows for training (and for the final refit over train + validation), all active rows for validation and test.

## 5.10 Final Modeling Dataset

Customer ID, snapshot date, segment flag (one-time / repeat), population flags (`in_training_sample`, `in_scoring_population`), sample weight, historical features, churn label and split assignment, produced by reproducible transformations from a single configuration.

# 7. Evaluation

## 7.1 Evaluation Framework

Evaluate the final system on the unseen future test period using three layers: **Predictive Performance**, **Targeting Performance** and **Business Impact Estimation**. Results are reported overall and for the one-time and repeat segments.

All evaluation uses the **Scoring Population**: every active customer at every weekly test date. Because the historical data contain no campaigns, suppression is not applied in the main evaluation. An optional sensitivity analysis can simulate suppression by removing customers selected in the Top-K during the previous S weeks.

The same customer appears in several consecutive test weeks, so test rows are not independent. This mirrors real operations and is acceptable, but uncertainty is reported across weeks (mean ± standard deviation) or with a customer-level bootstrap rather than as if rows were independent.

## 7.2 Classification Performance

ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Precision, Recall and F1. Always report the test churn rate next to PR-AUC as its random reference. **Calibration** (reliability curve, Brier score) is required whenever probabilities are used as values, e.g. expected revenue at risk; Random Forest and XGBoost may need Platt or isotonic calibration fitted on the validation period.

## 7.3 Ranking Performance

Precision\@K, Recall\@K, Lift\@K and cumulative Gain. Because targeting happens **each week**, these metrics are computed **per scoring date** — rank that week's full Scoring Population, cut the top K% — and then summarized as mean ± standard deviation across test weeks. Pooled curves may be shown additionally but are not the primary result.

## 7.4 Risk Band Analysis

Compare actual churn rates and customer characteristics across risk bands (e.g. deciles) to check that higher predicted risk corresponds to higher observed churn.

## 7.5 Customer Revenue at Risk

Estimate potential future revenue associated with churners, using a customer-level proxy:

::: {.formula}
AvgPurchaseValue~i~ = TotalRevenue~i~ ÷ PurchaseOccasions~i~  
RevenueAtRisk~i~ = AvgPurchaseValue~i~  (one future purchase-equivalent)  
or, with enough history: RevenueAtRisk~i~ = AvgMonthlyRevenue~i~ × H ÷ 30
:::

All inputs use history up to the snapshot date only. These are estimates of revenue exposure, not observed lost revenue.

## 7.6 Business Impact Scenario

For each target share K ∈ {10, 20, 30, 40, 50%}, per scoring week: customers targeted (N~K~), true churners captured (TP~K~), non-churners targeted (FP~K~), Recall\@K, and revenue at risk among targeted churners.

## 7.7 Retention Effectiveness Assumption

Retention success is an assumption, not an observed result. Scenarios: **Conservative e = 30%**, **Base e = 40%**, **Optimistic e = 50%**.

::: {.formula}
RevenuePreserved = RevenueAtRisk(targeted churners) × e
:::

## 7.8 Marketing Cost and the Value of the Model

If contact cost per customer is roughly constant, targeting K% instead of everyone reduces reach cost by (1 − K). This saving comes from **targeting itself**, not from the model — any ranking at the same K saves the same amount.

The model's contribution is measured by comparing, **at the same K**, the churners captured, revenue preserved and net impact of:

1. the final model,
2. the recency-rule baseline, and
3. random targeting.

## 7.9 Net Business Impact

Because preserved revenue is not profit, a margin assumption converts it before subtracting costs:

::: {.formula}
GrossProfitPreserved = RevenuePreserved × m  
ContactCost = N~K~ × c~contact~  
IncentiveCost = (TP~K~ × e + FP~K~ × r) × c~inc~  
NetBusinessImpact = GrossProfitPreserved − ContactCost − IncentiveCost
:::

Here *r* is the assumed share of targeted non-churners who redeem an incentive although they would have purchased anyway (cannibalization). Optionally report GrossProfitPreserved ÷ (ContactCost + IncentiveCost).

## 7.11 Model Comparison

Compare candidates (including baselines) on predictive metrics, ranking metrics, calibration, operational targeting capacity and business-impact scenarios. Do not define a single "best" model from classification metrics alone when the objective depends on ranking and cost constraints.

## 7.12 Error Analysis and Limitations

Investigate false positives, false negatives, segments (one-time vs repeat, UK vs non-UK, wholesale-sized customers) and weeks where performance differs, including seasonal effects. ::: {.principle}

:::

# 8. Deployment

::: {.note}
This chapter describes a **proposed operational design**. It is not implemented as a live system in this project.
:::

## 8.1 Deployment Objective

Provide recurring churn scoring that produces a prioritized existing-customer population for retention marketing.

## 8.2 Weekly Batch Pipeline

The training-episode cooldown is **not** part of this pipeline; it exists only in training-data construction.

::: {.flow}
Latest transactions → Valid transactions → Active customers (Recency ≤ R~max~) → Remove suppressed customers → Snapshots → Features → Scoring → Ranking → Capacity & business rules → Target population
:::

## 8.3 Customer Risk Output

Minimum output: `customer_id`, `snapshot_date`, `churn_probability`, `risk_band`, `rank`, `segment`, `model_version`.

## 8.4 Target Population

Select target customers based on churn risk, marketing capacity and business constraints.

## 8.5 Suppression

Customers **contacted** in a campaign are excluded from the Scoring Population for a configurable suppression period *S* (e.g. 4–8 weeks), so they are not targeted repeatedly. Suppression:

- applies only to customers who were actually targeted, not to everyone who was scored;
- is an operational business rule, independent of the training-episode cooldown *C*;
- is logged, so that suppressed customers can be analyzed separately once campaign data exist.

## 8.6 Monitoring

Data quality, feature drift, model performance, calibration, lift, risk-band behavior and business metrics where available.

# 10. Conclusion

## 10.1 Summary of Strategy

The project connects e-commerce revenue generation with customer retention and churn prediction.

## 10.2 Business-to-Technical Translation

The business objective is to increase sales revenue through repeat-purchase revenue from existing customers while controlling retention and marketing cost. The technical solution is customer-level churn prediction and risk ranking.

## 10.3 Operational Strategy

Weekly batch scoring of all active customers, customer snapshots, episode-based sampling for training, suppression of contacted customers, churn prediction, ranking and Top-K targeting.

## 10.4 Limitations

The dataset lacks marketing treatment, cost, margin and control-group information, so causal uplift, incremental revenue and true ROI cannot be established. The ~2-year span and seasonality limit the size and representativeness of the test period.

## 10.5 Future Work

Integrate campaign and financial data, run treatment/control experiments, apply uplift modeling and next-best-action optimization, and perform complete profitability analysis.
